      How this works and what it cannot do

      Two ways to read an enquiry

      • Analyze with Claude sends the enquiry text to Claude through your own Claude account and asks for structured results. Each detail comes back as stated, inferred or missing, with the exact words from the enquiry as proof.
      • Quick analyze never leaves this page. It uses fixed text patterns (numbers with units, currency amounts, delivery wording, city names, dates). It works well on plain, well-written enquiries and misses a lot on anything else.

      How stated, inferred and missing are decided

      • Stated by buyer: the buyer wrote it. The page checks that the quoted words really appear in the enquiry. If Claude claims something is stated but the quote is not found, the page downgrades it to inferred.
      • Inferred: the tool is guessing from context, for example a city that is mentioned but not described as the delivery place.
      • Missing: nothing in the text. Prices, units and dates are never filled in from guesses.

      Priority rules

      • Six details are worth points: quantity 20, product 15, specifications 15, budget 10, delivery location 10, deadline 10. Stated in full counts 100%, stated but vague counts 50%, inferred counts 25%, missing counts 0%.
      • Signals add points: urgent timeline 10, repeat or bulk need 5, contact details shared 5.
      • 70 or more is High, 40 to 69 is Medium, below 40 is Low. No product found caps the result at Low. No quantity and no specifications caps it at Medium.
      • The rules measure how ready the enquiry is to quote and how soon the buyer needs it. They say nothing about whether the buyer is real.

      Limits

      • It reads only the text you paste. It does not look up the buyer, your stock, prices or any marketplace data, and it does not contact anyone.
      • Relative deadlines such as "within 10 days" are counted from when the buyer wrote, which the text rarely says.
      • Festival and event deadlines such as "before Diwali" are kept as written and flagged. No date is assumed.
      • The draft reply is a template built from the stated details. It makes no promise about price, stock or lead time. Fill the [bracketed] parts yourself and read it before sending.
      • Text in other languages or very informal spelling may be misread, mostly in Quick analyze.
